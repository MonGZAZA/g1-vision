exp05: G1 Shop Greeter, student guide

Friday 13 November 2026 · True Digital Park West, 2nd floor

A Unitree G1 humanoid stands at a shop entrance. It greets customers with a Thai wai, introduces the shop, answers questions and points at booths. Today you run the system in simulation on your own laptop, then plan with your group how to develop your topic.

System overview

exp05 controls the upper body only. Unitree's own controller keeps the legs standing, and exp05 never commands them. The waist yaw (a small torso turn) and both arms go through Unitree's rt/arm_sdk channel, and the hands through the Dex3-1 hand topics. The simulation answers on the same DDS topics as the real robot, so the same server drives both.

1.1 Motion: Greeting (Sawasdee / wai)

10:00–10:40built in sim

When a customer arrives, the robot raises its hands into a wai and says "สวัสดีค่ะ" during the hold. It then lowers its hands and introduces itself, with gestures. The wai motion was learned from a video, not keyframed by hand.

Try it

  1. Operate → 🙏 Sawasdee + introduce. Watch the MuJoCo window. Switch the header language between ไทย and EN and run it again.
  2. Move the speed slider (Upper body card) to 0.3, then to 1.0, and compare. Speed 0.5 is the default for a first test on the real robot.
  3. Open Scripts & voice → Motion learned from video. The wai entry shows its checks: palm gap, symmetry and peak joint speed. Untick it to compare with the procedural wai.
  4. Read the clip: clips/wai_dex3.json holds t, q (15 joints: waist yaw + 2 × 7 arm), hands, phases and checks.
  5. Optional, with the AI install: record your own wai on your phone and load it with Teach from a new video (see the video specs below). The old clip is kept: Keep the old one or Undo brings it back.

How it works

  1. Video → measurements (ai/tools/video_to_wai.py): MediaPipe Holistic tracks the body and both hands in each frame. The tool finds the wai holds and measures the hold height (fingertips relative to the shoulders and chin), the forward tilt of the hands, the finger straightness and the timing.
  2. Measurements → robot clip (g1/tools/make_wai_clip.py, gestures.solve_wai): inverse kinematics on the robot model. The fingertips go to about 1.17 m (chin height on the G1). The second arm mirrors the first, so the wai is symmetric. A monocular video usually cuts off the elbows, so the arm angles come from IK, not from the human's joint angles.
  3. Playback (behaviour.py → intro): one timeline holds the motion clip and the speech. The clip runs at 50 Hz with minimum-jerk segments, and peak joint speeds stay under 80 % of the channel limit (2 rad/s × speed).
Dex3 wai, changed 13 Nov: the first version was judged below standard in the sim review. On the Dex3-1 hands the folded thumb bases stand about 3.2 cm proud of the finger pads, so with the palms facing each other the thumb bases touched first and the palms stayed about 9 cm apart. Now each hand turns 35° inward about the vertical (gestures.WAI_HAND_TURN_DEG). The finger edges close in front, about 3.7 cm apart, and the thumb bases move back toward the chest. The nearest hand parts are 1.4 cm apart (checks.palm_gap_min_m in clips/wai_dex3.json), so the hands never press. The Inspire 5-finger hands meet palm to palm with no turn (HANDS=inspire ./run_sim.sh). The new wai has been checked in simulation only, not on the real robot.
Robot constraints: our G1's waist roll and pitch are locked, so the robot cannot bow: the wai is hands only. The Dex3-1 thumb sticks out about 8.8 cm when open, so it is folded across the palm. Otherwise the two thumbs would collide.
Video specs for a good wai or keep-alive recording
  • One person, front view, camera at chest height, 2–3 m away, on a tripod (no hand-held camera).
  • The whole upper body and both hands in the frame for the whole time, with plain clothes and a plain background.
  • Wai: stand still, 2–3 slow wais with a 1–2 s hold each. Keep-alive: 30–60 s of standing and talking as a greeter.
  • H.264 (avc1) MP4. AV1 video does not decode in the tool.

Discussion for Lead / Observer

  • Dex3 hand turn: is the 35° turn a convincing wai to Thai customers? A larger turn closes the fingers more (55° gives about 2.3 cm), but then the palms face backward. Compare HANDS=dex3 with HANDS=inspire (palm to palm) and decide whether to wait for the Inspire hands.
  • Quality: how do we judge a "good" wai? Compare it side by side with a Thai person. Add a numeric metric, such as hold height, palm contact or symmetry, and a check by people.
  • Bow: the waist is locked. Is a small stand-height dip through Unitree's API acceptable and safe, or is a wai with the hands only enough?
  • Variants: a different wai for different people (a customer, an elder, a monk)? A shorter greeting for a returning customer, which links to 2.3?
  • Real robot: plan the first hardware test (TESTING_ROBOT.md steps 1–5 and 8). Who is the operator and who is the spotter?
  • Data: who records our own wai and keep-alive videos, and how do we get consent for using them?

1.2 Motion: Speaking Gestures

10:40–11:20built in sim

A robot that stands frozen while it talks looks broken. exp05 has two motion layers. An idle (keep-alive) layer is always on: slow breathing, small arm sway, finger drift and a rare 3° glance with the waist. On top of it, talking gestures are placed on the speech timeline.

Try it

  1. Operate → Introduce service. Watch when the gestures start. They are aligned to the phrase starts that the TTS reports.
  2. Untick keep-alive idle, wait a few seconds, then tick it again. Notice how dead the robot looks without it.
  3. In Scripts & voice, move or add tags in the service text, Save texts, then run Introduce service again. The gesture tags are listed in the table below.
  4. Make it say any text with gestures from a terminal: 
    curl -s localhost:8100/api/say -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"lang":"en","text":"[welcome] Hello everyone! [present] Today we build a greeter robot. [both] Let us start."}'
Tag / gestureMotionWhen
[welcome]both open palms out to the sides, palms upgreeting, "welcome"
[present]one open palm forward-out, palm up. It alternates left and right"here is…", "we have…"
[both]both hands presentlists, "everything"
beat (automatic)a small forearm lift with a relaxed handat phrase starts, every second phrase, alternating arms
idle (always)sines on each joint, breathing on the shoulders, finger drift, a rare waist glanceall the time, also on top of other clips

How it works

  • scripts.py stores the texts (defaults plus each shop's own in shop.json). split_tags separates the spoken text from the tags.
  • behaviour.py → talk: the TTS returns phrases with start and end times. Each tag or beat becomes a gesture at its phrase start, held while the phrase lasts, then the arm returns toward rest.
  • gestures.py: the gesture poses are solved once with IK and cached in cache/poses_dex3.json. idle() holds the amplitudes and frequencies of the keep-alive layer. A style learned from a video (ai/tools/video_to_idle.py --install → clips/idle.json) can scale them.
  • Safety in the motion layer: joint limits with a margin, speed limits, and hands kept inside a box in front of the body, so they never swing far toward people.

Discussion for Lead / Observer

  • Automatic tags: the LLM answers in 2.1 have no tags today, so they only get beats. Could the LLM, or a simple keyword rule, insert [present] and the other tags?
  • Style: the example presenter videos were too lively for a shop greeter. Which keep-alive video do we record, and how calm should the robot be?
  • Variety: the same three gestures repeat. Should we add more, such as a "this way" sweep or counting on the fingers? How many before it looks random?
  • Listening state: what should the body do while a customer speaks (links to 2.2)? For example, a slight lean and turn toward the person, no beats, and a "thinking" pose while the LLM runs.
  • Balance on the real robot: arm motion shifts the centre of mass. The sim shows a ZMP margin of 5 cm or more. Who checks this on the real robot, and how?

1.3 Motion: Pointing & Booth Configuration

11:20–12:00built in sim

The operator, or later the dialogue, asks "where is the cafe?". The robot turns its torso a little and points with an open palm, palm up, the polite way. For a near booth the arm is low; for a far one it is nearly horizontal. The robot then says "คาเฟ่อยู่ทางนี้ค่ะ" as the arm arrives. The booths come from a shop map that you edit in the browser.

Try it

  1. Operate → Point to a booth: try a near booth (New Arrivals, 3 m), a side booth (Cafe, Restrooms) and a far booth (Service Center, 27 m). Compare the arm height.
  2. Open the Shop tab. Click Copy to make your own shop, so you don't change mockup. Then:
    • Robot: click to place it, drag to set its heading.
    • + Booth: drag a rectangle; name it in Thai and English in the side panel.
    • Drag the red aim point to where the hand should point.
    • Put a booth behind the robot and watch it turn grey: the robot cannot point there.
  3. Save (Ctrl+S), then Use for robot, and point at your new booths from the Operate tab.
  4. Bring a real floor plan: Upload an image. Use Scale (click two points and type their real distance) and Origin, then place the robot and the booths.
  5. From a terminal: 
    curl -s localhost:8100/api/point/plans | python3 -m json.tool | head -40     # every booth's plan
curl -s localhost:8100/api/point -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d '{"booth":"cafe","lang":"th","hold_s":4}'
  6. The accuracy check: cd g1 && .venv/bin/python tools/point_check.py (15 checks; it covers 12 booths plus 168 grid targets).

How it works (g1/deploy/pointing.py)

  1. Booth aim point → azimuth θ and distance d as seen by the robot (the shop frame is in metres; the robot pose comes from shop.json).
  2. Arm choice: the arm on that side; straight ahead uses the right arm. Torso: waist yaw = 0.25 × θ, at most 15°. The arm does the rest.
  3. Elevation from distance: −40° at 1.5 m up to −3° at 40 m. Near booths get a low arm, which also reads as "right here".
  4. IK: the wrist goes on the ray from the shoulder, the fingers along the ray and the palm turned up 50°, with the elbow slightly bent. Position is solved first, then orientation, from several seeds. Targets out to ±100° are reachable. A booth further behind is greyed with the reason.
  5. Results: the worst azimuth error is 3.5° over all test targets, and in the sim the ray is within 0.5°. Plans are cached per shop.

Shop file (shops/<name>/shop.json): robot {x, y, yaw_deg}, booths[] with id, name_th/en, rect, aim {x,y,z}, description_th/en, labels (synonyms for Q&A), plus faq[] and scripts. x points right and y into the shop, in metres; yaw 90° = up on the map.

Discussion for Lead / Observer

  • Real shop: who measures the venue, and how? Options: a floor plan with known scale, a tape measure, or the lidar map from exp04 (FAST-LIO2). How accurately must the robot's own position and heading be known? A 5° heading error moves the pointing target 1.7 m at 20 m.
  • Verification on the real robot: how do we measure where it actually points, for example with a laser pointer taped to the palm, or a camera from above?
  • Booths behind the robot: say "behind me" without pointing, or turn more? The legs stay under Unitree's control, so the robot does not turn on the spot.
  • From dialogue to pointing: today Q&A finds the booth by name or label. Which synonyms and descriptions does each booth need? This is shared with 2.1.
  • People nearby: the arm sweeps sideways. Should the robot check, with 2.3, that nobody is within 1 m before it points?

  2. Scripts & voice → Voice: try the speaking speed (0.8–1.2×) and the volume. Watch the captions follow the speed.
  3. Optional: record a reference voice in the browser (Record, read the script aloud, Upload). Thai is then spoken in that voice. Only clone your own voice, or a voice whose owner has agreed.
  4. Call the APIs directly:

  | python3 -c "import sys,json; d=json.load(sys.stdin); print(d['duration'], d['phrases'])"
curl -s localhost:8105/health
  5. Add your own FAQ entry: edit "faq" in shops/<your shop>/shop.json (q_th, q_en, a_th, a_en, optional booth), then press Use for robot again in the Shop tab. The page has no FAQ editor yet.

How it works

  • Q&A (g1/deploy/qa.py):
    1. Language from the letters: Thai if the question has Thai characters, else English.
    2. FAQ: if the string similarity is ≥ 0.72, the stored answer is used.
    3. Otherwise the LLM gets a prompt with the shop context: names, service text, every booth with its description and where it is as seen from the entrance, and the FAQ. It writes a short answer.
    4. Booth: the FAQ entry's booth, else a booth whose name or label appears in the question or the answer.
  • Speech (speech.py): the TTS output is cached in cache/tts/, and fixed texts are pre-rendered when a shop becomes active. The audio starts on the motion clip's clock minus the output's latency, so the caption, voice and gesture start together (within ±60 ms in the sim).
  • The operator stays in the loop: answers are shown and can be edited before they are spoken. That is deliberate while the LLM is untested with real customers.

Discussion for Lead / Observer

  • Voice: which voice do we choose? Use a cloned voice from a real person (consent, recording quality), or the default? Test it with listeners at the real entrance noise level.
  • Pronunciation: check Thai numbers, times, prices, brand names and English words inside Thai sentences. Do we need a text normalisation step before the TTS?
  • Dialogue design: today each answer is one-shot, with no memory. Do we need multi-turn context ("and how much is it?"), clarifying questions, and handing over to staff?
  • Safety of answers: the LLM must not invent prices or promotions. Options: stricter prompts, FAQ-only mode, answer checking, logging, and when to skip the operator check.
  • Latency budget: question → answer → speech should take under about 2 s. The filler "สักครู่นะคะ" exists for slower cases. Measure it on the target laptop with 2.2 running too, because they share the GPU.
  • Evaluation: build a test set of real customer questions in Thai and English with expected answers and booths, and track accuracy.

2.2 Speech: Speech Recognition

13:40–14:20built, sim (2026-11-13)
The robot can listen. Operate tab → Listen card: Push-to-talk (hold 🎤 or the space bar) or Auto-listen. The recognised question goes to the existing Q&A, and the robot answers (and points when the answer names a booth). Real voices on the real microphone are still untested: that is this session's job.

How it works

mic (laptop: parec 16 kHz | robot: G1 multicast, untested) ─▶ 32 ms frames
  ─▶ muted while the robot speaks (from the start of an answer to its end + 0.4 s)
  ─▶ Silero VAD v6 (ONNX, CPU): a segment ends after 0.7 s of silence (max 12 s)
  ─▶ AI service POST :8105/asr  (typhoon-ai/typhoon-whisper-turbo, Thai; it also writes English; ~1.7 GB GPU)
  ─▶ filters: too short · low confidence · phantom phrases ("ขอบคุณที่รับชม") · Auto-listen: "not a question for the robot"
  ─▶ reply: Q&A (qa.answer) → /api/say or /api/point     (or "put in the question box" for the operator)
  • Code: g1/deploy/listen.py (mic, VAD, gates, filters, reply), ai/service.py (ASR class, POST /asr), the Listen card in static/index.html.
  • API: POST /api/listen {mode off|ptt|auto, reply auto|review, relevance, presence_gate}, GET /api/listen (state, mic level, last 20 texts), POST /api/listen/ptt {down}, POST /api/listen/presence {present, hold_s} (from vision 2.3: PRESENCE=1 ./run_vision.sh), POST /api/listen/audio (a 16-bit mono WAV, heard as if from the mic).
  • Check: cd g1 && .venv/bin/python tools/listen_check.py (18 checks; needs the AI service with ASR). It speaks the questions with the robot's own TTS, so it checks the plumbing, not the accuracy.
  • Known limits: the synthetic Thai voice is a poor test (it slurs "ห้องน้ำ" into "ห้องนอน" even when the audio goes straight to the model). On this laptop the default input is the empty headset jack, so the listener picks the next live input (the built-in "Digital Microphone"). The robot's own microphone path is written but untested. With the LLM, the whole reply took ~3.4 s, above the 2 s budget.

Starter exercise (about 15 min)

  1. Start the sim (./run_sim.sh), open the Operate tab, and choose Push-to-talk with reply "put in the question box". Ask 5 questions in Thai and 2 in English. Note each wrong text and the recognition time shown in the list.
  2. Record 3 questions as WAV files (16-bit mono) and post them with curl --data-binary @q.wav localhost:8100/api/listen/audio. Then do it again with shop noise mixed in.
  3. Switch to Auto-listen and talk near the robot without asking it anything. Does "only questions" keep it quiet?

  • Test set: who records 50–100 typical questions from several speakers, with background noise?

2.3 Vision: Human Detection & Recognition

14:20–15:00built in sim (13 Nov)

The vision module (vision/, port 8107) sees the people around the robot. It reports where each person is, whether they face the robot, how fast they walk and whether anyone is too close to the arms. Vision only reports: it does not decide what the robot does. A behaviour state machine, still to be built, turns these reports into greet, listen and point actions. Details: vision/README.md.

What it does

  • Camera: MediaPipe pose finds up to 6 people per frame. A YuNet face detector on each head crop decides facing: a frontal face with the head turned less than 40°.
  • Head lidar (MID-360): each camera person is matched to the lidar cluster in the same direction, which gives the distance (about 2 cm error in the sim). People beside or behind the robot, outside the camera view, are tracked by the lidar alone.
  • Tracks: Kalman filters give a stable id, the walking speed and whether the person is approaching.
  • Safety: a 360° too_close warning from the lidar when anyone is within 1 m. The robot's own arms are filtered out using the posed robot model, so pointing or a wai doesn't trigger it.
  • Runs on the CPU or GPU (about 25–170 ms per frame) with almost no GPU memory. It works without the AI install.

Try it

  1. Start the robot stack (./run_sim.sh), then in a second terminal: 
    cd vision && ./run_vision.sh     # sensor sim (walking mannequins + lidar + camera) + vision service
./run_vision.sh webcam             # or: your laptop webcam, no lidar (distance from the image, rough)

Camera on the real robot

If our robot's head camera is mounted like Unitree's model says, vision can't see faces, and facing stays null. Distance, speed, proximity and lidar tracking still work. Options, in order of effort:

  1. check one real image from the head camera;
  2. add a level USB camera next to the head D435i, used only for detection and facing (details below);
  3. re-aim the D435i, only with the robot owner's agreement.
Option 2 in detail: a level USB camera
  • Camera: a plain USB (UVC) webcam, 720p at 30 fps, with a 70–90° field of view (Logitech C920 / C930e class, or a small Arducam UVC board). These work with OpenCV on Linux without drivers. Avoid fisheye lenses (over 120°). Depth isn't needed: the lidar gives the distance.
  • Place: on top of or in front of the head, 1.25–1.35 m above the floor, looking level (level: faces from about 1.2 m to 6 m) or 10° down (tilt10: better at 1–1.5 m). A chest mount works too, but the arms pass in front of it when pointing and in the wai. Use a clamp or 3D-printed bracket with no screws into the robot, and route the cable away from the arms.
  • Connect: A, USB to the laptop with an active extension (5–10 m), which is the recommended first step: CAMERA=webcam:N ./run_vision.sh robot. B, USB to the robot's PC2 with exp04's camera_streamer.py, then CAMERA=http://192.168.123.164:8093/color.jpg. PC2 is shared, so B needs the other team's agreement.
  • Set up (about 10 min): measure the camera's position and angle and pass CAMERA_MOUNT=x,y,z,roll,pitch,yaw (metres, degrees) and HFOV. A few cm or degrees of error is fine. Check by standing at 1, 2 and 3 m in front of the robot: the camera box and the lidar cluster should agree (source = lidar+camera).
  • Not built yet: automatic calibration of the camera's yaw and pitch from a person walking in front of the robot.
Privacy and law: vision stores no images and does no face recognition. The live camera page is on 127.0.0.1 only. Face recognition would be biometric data under Thailand's PDPA: only for people who opted in, and agreed with the shop first. Research notes: research/2.3_human_detection.md.

Discussion for Lead / Observer

  • Camera: which of the three camera options? First check one real head-camera image. If it only shows legs, option 2 (a level USB camera) is the quick path: who buys and mounts the webcam, which route (laptop or PC2), and who measures CAMERA_MOUNT? A possible student task: auto-calibrate the camera angle from the lidar.
  • Real data: it has only been tested in the sim. Expect false clusters (bags, trolleys, children, glass) and a self-filter margin to tune (cables, the real hands). How do we record a test set at the entrance, and with whose permission? Stage it with group members first.
  • Thresholds: near at 2.0 m, dwell after 2 s, facing within 40°, too close at 1 m. Are these right for a real shop entrance? How do we measure false greetings per hour?
  • State machine owner: the event contract now exists (above). Who builds the behaviour layer that consumes it (greet, listen, point, cooldowns), and where does it run? See the integration section.
  • Compute: vision runs on the laptop next to the TTS, LLM and ASR on the same GPU. Its frame time grows to 100–400 ms while the AI service is busy. Is that enough, or do we use CPU=1 or move it to the robot's PC2?
  • Recognition: do we need it at all, for example staff by uniform rather than face? If yes, the consent process comes first.

3. Shared rules to agree on

  • Ports: 8100 server (with ASR, /api/listen*), 8105 AI service (with /asr), 8107 vision (8110 vision sensor sim, UDP 56305 sim lidar). Proposed: 8108 behaviour state machine. Tests use 8101–8104 and 8109.
  • GPU budget on the robot laptop (8 GB today): TTS + LLM + cloning + ASR take ~5.0 GB. Vision must fit in the rest, or move to another machine.
  • Safety: only one program commands the arms. The E-STOP releases the arms and never switches to damping. Nothing runs on the real robot without the TESTING_ROBOT.md procedure, an operator and a spotter holding the Unitree remote.
  • Code: one shared repository. Each group works in its own folder or service; changes to g1/deploy/server.py go through one owner. Each module ships with a check script, like g1/tools/*_check.py.
  • Data and privacy: consent for voice clones, videos and recordings. Detection only by default (PDPA).
  • Demo target: agree on one end-to-end scenario for the next joint review, for example: a person walks up → wai + introduction → asks "ห้องน้ำอยู่ไหน" by voice → the robot answers and points.
